# The 17 standard questions

Nearly every process asks some version of these. Six are company-specific and get tailored in each prep. The other eleven are about the candidate and come from `profile/interview-context.md`; they are rehearsed once and reused. Tailoring them again per company wastes prep time and produces a document the candidate will not read.

| # | Question | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tell me about yourself | Tailored |
| 2 | Why are you looking for a new role? | Tailored (framing from role-context "why-leaving") |
| 3 | Why are you interested in this position? | Tailored |
| 4 | What do you know about our company? | Tailored |
| 5 | What are you looking for in your next role? | interview-context |
| 6 | Tell me about your current (or most recent) responsibilities | interview-context, pre-load one tradeoff |
| 7 | What is your biggest strength? | interview-context |
| 8 | Where do you see room for personal growth? | interview-context |
| 9 | What are you most proud of professionally? | interview-context |
| 10 | Tell me about a challenge you faced | interview-context, end with the tradeoff |
| 11 | What gets you excited about your work? | interview-context |
| 12 | What kind of environment helps you do your best work? | interview-context |
| 13 | What do you value in leadership? | interview-context |
| 14 | What are your salary expectations? | Tailored |
| 15 | What is your notice period / availability? | Tailored |
| 16 | Where do you see yourself in 2 to 3 years? | interview-context |
| 17 | Do you have any questions for me? | Tailored, per interviewer |

If the most recent role has ended, Q6 becomes "most recent responsibilities" and is answered in the past tense.

## Key beats for the tailored six

### Q1: Tell me about yourself (about 75 seconds)

Three movements, in bullets:
- **Present:** title and domain in one line, then the two or three things the most recent role was about. Include one tradeoff decision; engineering listeners hear it as "understands that decisions have costs".
- **Past proof:** one earlier story with a number that the interviewer's lens cares about (see the analysis for what the hiring manager wants to de-risk).
- **Why here:** one sentence connecting the above to this role. Not the Q3 answer, just the bridge.

Stop at 75 seconds. The interviewer will pull on the thread they care about.

### Q2: Why are you looking?

Use the why-leaving framing from `profile/role-context.md` for the current or most recent role. Honest, short, forward-looking. If the role ended (redundancy, restructure, contract end), say so plainly in one clause and spend the rest on what the candidate wants next. Nobody is penalised for a restructure; people are penalised for sounding evasive about it.

### Q3: Why this position? (three reasons)

Three numbered reasons, each one line, each anchored in something the analysis found (the business problem behind the hire, the product line, the team's stage). Avoid reasons that apply to any company ("great culture", "exciting product"). Do not repeat the strongest-angles section; this is why the candidate wants them, not why they should want the candidate.

### Q4: What do you know about us?

Four to six beats: what the company does in plain terms, who the customers are, the recent moves that explain why this role exists now, and one piece of domain context (the industry shift, the regulatory change, the competitor move). Domain context is what separates "read the website" from "has started thinking like an insider". Close with one open question that shows the homework ("I could not tell from outside whether X; is that right?").

### Q14: Salary expectations

Different plays by stage.

**Recruiter screen: flip the question first.**

> "Before I share a number, what is the range you have been briefed on for this role? Then I can tell you whether we are in the same ballpark."

Recruiters usually have a band. Hearing it first means the candidate does not anchor below budget.

Before the call, check the specific company on Glassdoor and Levels.fyi, not the generic title median for the city. Sector matters more than title: a venture-backed software company, a multinational in a non-tech sector, and a listed bank pay very differently for the same title. Do not read a total-compensation figure as base salary; that mistake over-anchors by 30 to 50 percent and gets candidates screened out before the hiring manager call.

If pushed for a number:

> "For a <level> <function> role with this scope, I would be looking at <sector-appropriate range> base, plus the right bonus and equity structure. I am happy to flex on the mix."

If they come in below the anchor:

> "That is below where I need to land for a role at this level. Is there flex in the band, or is the level negotiable?"

**Hiring manager or later:** usually deferred to the recruiter. If asked, give the same range and move on.

Record the band in `compensation.md` and `tracker.json` (`salary-band-confirmed`) the moment it is disclosed.

### Q15: Notice period and availability

From `profile/interview-context.md`. State notice, earliest start, any fixed constraints. Mention work authorisation only if `interview-context.md` says to; otherwise leave it for the recruiter's own question.

### Q17: Questions for them

Per interviewer, in their lens, two or three each, plus one best opener for the first interviewer. Prefer questions that:
- turn a research inference into a question (signals homework);
- ask about the hiring manager's first-six-months outcome;
- ask what the last person in the role found hardest;
- for recruiters, ask about process, timeline, and the band.

Avoid questions answered on the careers page.
