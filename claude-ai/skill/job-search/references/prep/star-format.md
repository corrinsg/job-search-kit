# STAR bank format (interview-qa.md)

A STAR bank is six to ten worked examples the candidate can reach for across a loop. Written once, referenced from every round. Each example is built from a story in `profile/role-context.md`, including its tradeoff, because engineering and product interviewers want the cost story as much as the win.

```
# <Company>: <Role> | Example bank

## Allocation
| Round | Competency | Primary example | Backup |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | <Case study> | Example 2 | Example 5 |
| 2 | <Execution> | Example 1 | Example 4 |

## Example 1 | <Principle it demonstrates> (<Employer>)
**Problem:** <What was broken or missing, who felt it, the number if there is one.>
**The competing priorities:** <The two or three things pulling in different directions.>
**What I did:** <The decisions, in order, in first person. Named alternatives considered.>
**The tradeoff I accepted:** <What was given up to get the result, and the condition under which the other choice would have been right.>
**Results:** <The outcome with the number, and the second-order effect.>
*Say it:* *"<One-sentence spoken version that opens the story in the room.>"*

## Example 2 | ...
```

## Choosing the examples

- Cover every competency the loop tests at least once; the analysis and the company's interview guide tell you which.
- Prefer stories with a defensible number and a real tradeoff. A win story without a cost reads as "does not understand the decision" to technical listeners.
- Follow the story ratio in `profile/preferences.md`: the most recent employer carries most examples.
- Use interview-only facts from `role-context.md` freely; never-claim items not at all.
- Each example should be tellable in 90 seconds from the "Say it" line. If it needs more than six lines under "What I did", split it or cut it.

## Question annotations

When a round section lists likely questions, annotate what kind of answer each needs, so the candidate does not give a story where a view was wanted:

- **(POV)**: a point of view, two or three lines of opinion with one reason.
- **(framework)**: how the candidate would approach it, structure first.
- **(framework + example)**: structure, then one example from the bank.
- **(POV + honest boundary)**: an opinion plus where the candidate's experience stops.

## Ending every story

The last line of a story answer should be the result and the tradeoff, in that order. If the interviewer goes quiet after the result, they may be waiting for the cost. Say it before they have to ask.
