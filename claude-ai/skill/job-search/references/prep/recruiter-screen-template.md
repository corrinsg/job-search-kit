# Recruiter screen template

A recruiter screen is a filter, not a conversation about craft. The recruiter has a sheet of boxes to tick and a band to confirm, and will pass along a two-line summary of the candidate. The prep's job is to make those two lines easy to write. 75 to 250 lines; "say it" sections are written to be spoken.

```
# <Company>: <Role> | Recruiter screen prep

**When:** <date, time, duration> | **Recruiter:** <name, title, agency or in-house> | **Fit:** <X/10>

## What the recruiter is actually trying to find (the boxes to tick)
- <Non-negotiable 1 from the reconstructed recruiter briefing in analysis.md>
- <Non-negotiable 2>
- <Level and scope check>
- <Salary band fit>
- <Availability and location>
- <"Will the hiring manager like this person" read>

## The company in 60 seconds
- <Four bullets: what, for whom, stage, recent move>

## About me, say it
<Five or six lines of first-person beats, spoken register. Present, one proof with a number, why here. This is Q1 trimmed for a recruiter who is not technical.>

## Why interested, say it
<Three short reasons in the candidate's words, each anchored in something specific about this company.>

## Q beats
- **Why looking:** <one or two lines from role-context why-leaving framing>
- **Current or most recent responsibilities:** <three beats, plain language>
- **Biggest strength:** <one line and the example>
- **Looking for next:** <one line>

## Competitors, and "why them, not those"
- <Competitor>: <one line on the difference>
- <Competitor>: ...
- Why this company over those: <one line>

## Salary
<The flip-the-question play, the company-specific band research in one line, the if-pushed phrasing, the if-below phrasing. See standard-questions.md Q14.>

## Notice and availability
<From interview-context.md.>

## Location
<Hybrid or remote expectations as the JD states them, and the candidate's position.>

## Handle honestly (do not over-volunteer)
<Two or three topics where a short honest answer is right and a long one invites a problem: a gap in the CV, a requirement not met, the reason a previous process ended. Give the one-line version of each.>

## Questions for the recruiter (pick 3 or 4)
- What does the process look like from here, and what is the timeline?
- What is the band you have been briefed on?
- What does the hiring manager care most about in this hire?
- Who else is on the panel?
- What has made previous candidates fall out of the process?

## Watch-outs
- <Three to five: things this recruiter or process may do, and how to respond>

## Pre-call checklist
- [ ] Band research open
- [ ] Notice and start date confirmed
- [ ] Two-line summary of myself ready
- [ ] <Specific item>

## Walk-in frame
<One sentence. Warm, concise, easy to summarise.>

## Debrief after the call
<Leave empty. Fill with: band disclosed, next steps, who is on the panel, anything the recruiter said about focus. Then run the debrief phase if there is a lesson, or just update tracker.json.>
```

## Notes

- Recruiters relay impressions, not arguments. Short, confident, consistent beats travel better than depth.
- If the recruiter is an agency recruiter, they may know less about the role than the candidate does after the analysis. Be generous with context and ask what they know about the hiring manager.
- Record the band and the panel names in `compensation.md` and `tracker.json` the same day.
