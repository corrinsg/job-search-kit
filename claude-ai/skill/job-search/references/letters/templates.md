# Message templates

Shapes, not scripts. Fill them from `analysis.md` and the profile; cut anything that does not carry a specific fact about this company or this candidate.

## Cover letter

```markdown
**Use case:** attached to the application for <Role> at <Company>
**Length:** <n> words
**Strategy:** <the hook in one sentence>

## The letter

<Paragraph 1, two or three sentences: the hook. Name the problem the role exists to solve (analysis Part 1, question 7) and the one piece of experience that speaks to it directly. No "I am writing to apply".>

<Paragraph 2, three or four sentences: the evidence. The two requirements the recruiter briefing ranks highest, each answered with a concrete proof point and a number from role-context.md.>

<Paragraph 3, optional, two sentences: the honest gap and the ramp. Or, when there is no gap worth raising, the second-order reason this company and this moment fit what the candidate wants next, from interview-context.md.>

<Closing, one or two sentences: the ask. "I would welcome a conversation about <specific topic>." Then the sign-off with the candidate's name.>

## Why this works

- Paragraph 1: <why this hook, which analysis line it comes from>
- Paragraph 2: <why these two proof points, which non-negotiables they answer>
- Paragraph 3: <why raise or not raise the gap>
- Close: <why this ask>

## What's deliberately not in the letter

- <The CV summary restated: the reader has the CV>
- <A gap not raised, and why it is better handled in conversation>
- <Anything the candidate cannot defend in detail>
```

## Cold outreach to a recruiter or hiring manager

Subject line (email) or first line (LinkedIn): the role title and one hook word.

```
Hi <first name>,

<One sentence: who the candidate is in terms of the role's core requirement, with a number.>
<One sentence: the specific thing about the company or the role that prompted the message, from analysis Part 1.>
<One sentence: the direct match, "the work in this posting is what I have been doing at <employer>: <one proof point>".>
<One sentence: the ask. "Would you be open to a short call this week or next?">

<Name>
```

Rules: under 120 words; no attachment pitch ("I have attached my CV"), the CV goes via the application or on request; one hook, not three.

## Reply to an inbound recruiter

```
Hi <first name>, thanks for reaching out.

<One sentence: interest level, honest. "This looks close to what I am looking for" or "I am not looking at <sector> roles right now, but...">
<One or two sentences: the two things the candidate needs to know before a call, drawn from interview-context.md: location or remote policy, level and scope, or the compensation band. Ask for the band here rather than naming a number: "Could you share the range you have been briefed on?">
<One sentence: availability for a call, with two concrete slots.>

<Name>
```

Rules: answer within a day; never name a salary figure in writing before the recruiter does; keep the candidate's leverage by stating interest without eagerness.

## Follow-up after silence

Send 7 to 10 days after the last contact, once. A second follow-up after another 10 days closes the thread politely.

```
Hi <first name>,

<One sentence: reference the application or last conversation with the date.>
<One sentence: a new, small, specific reason the role is still interesting, ideally something that happened at the company since (a launch, a hire, an announcement from analysis Part 1).>
<One sentence: the ask. "Is there anything else you need from me, or a sense of timing for next steps?">

<Name>
```

## Thank-you after an interview

Send the same day. One message per interviewer when it was a panel; each must differ.

```
Hi <first name>,

<One sentence: thanks, naming the conversation.>
<One or two sentences: the one topic from the interview worth extending: an idea the interviewer raised, a question the candidate wants to answer more fully, or a follow-up thought. This is the only part that matters.>
<One sentence: a plain statement of interest and the next step.>

<Name>
```

Rules: no restating the whole interview; no new sales pitch; if the candidate flubbed a question, one clean sentence correcting it is fine, more is not.

## Re-engagement after a rejection

Owned by `the debrief phase`, which has the template and the strategic angle. Do not duplicate it here.

## Tells to cut on a final read

- "I am writing to express my interest"
- "I was excited to see"
- "passionate about", "genuinely", "not just X, Y"
- "This role reads like", "the JD reads as"
- "proven track record", "unique blend"
- Any sentence that could be sent to another company unchanged.
