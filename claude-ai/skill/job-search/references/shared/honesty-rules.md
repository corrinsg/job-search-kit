# Honesty rules for tailoring

A tailored CV must survive a probing interview. Every rule here exists because the opposite shortcut was tempting, looked harmless on the page, and would have been exposed the moment an interviewer asked "tell me more about that".

## 1. Never fabricate bullets

Only write bullets describing things the candidate did. Reframing real work in the job description's vocabulary is fine. Inventing an achievement because the JD asked for it is not, however plausible it sounds for someone at that level.

Never invent without explicit confirmation from the candidate:
- Strategic decisions (invest, pivot, stop, consolidate)
- People management actions or team sizes
- Technical decisions (tool choices, migrations, architectures)
- Numbers or metrics
- Cross-team or stakeholder work at a specific scale
- Specific products or features shipped

If a JD asks for something the candidate has not done, the options are: leave it off (they have other strengths); address it honestly in the cover letter or first call ("my BI stack was X not Y, I would ramp on Y"); or find an adjacent achievement they did do. When in doubt, ask the candidate first and write the bullet second. The honest CV is also the safer CV: honest bullets can be defended in detail.

## 2. Do not stretch technical vocabulary to match the JD

If a JD word tempts you to relabel existing work, check whether the work matches that word's technical meaning. Terms with specific meanings:

| Term | Means | Does not mean |
|---|---|---|
| Integration | Connecting operational systems (iPaaS, API integration between apps, ESB) | Any data flowing between tools |
| Real-time | Sub-second latency | Streaming or event-driven in general |
| Platform | Something other teams build on | Every product surface |
| Cloud-native | Designed around cloud primitives (containers, managed services, elastic scale) | Runs on a cloud provider |
| Infrastructure | Foundational systems other things depend on | Every backend service |
| AI | ML or LLM in production | Data products that feed ML |

If the work does not match: use the accurate term in the bullet; surface the JD vocabulary in a different bullet where the work does match; and if nothing matches, let the summary carry the framing without forcing it into bullets. The summary can be framing; bullets are evidence and must be precise.

## 3. Do not claim a general capability from one example

When a JD asks for a general capability ("strong judgement about when to use AI versus deterministic approaches") and the candidate has one strong example, write the example factually and stop. Do not append a sentence that mirrors the JD and claims the general capability. One shipped project proves one decision, not a pattern. Tells to watch for as tail sentences on single-project bullets: "Informed judgement on...", "Demonstrated ability to...", "Built expertise in...", "Proven track record of...". Those phrases belong in a summary backed by several bullets, or in the interview where the candidate can show multiple cases.

## 4. Do not infer specifics from general facts

Two traps:

**General tool → specific tool.** "Databricks" does not mean Unity Catalog. "AWS" does not mean EKS. "Kafka" does not mean Confluent. "Airflow" does not mean a managed Airflow service. When only the general platform is known, use generic language ("the catalogue layer", "the orchestrator"), list the option space, and make "which do you use?" one of the first interview questions. That turns an inference into a signal of homework.

**Suggestive biography → confirmed continuity.** Long tenure at a consultancy near the company's HQ with similar-sounding project descriptions does not prove the interviewer worked on this company's account. Write "possibly", "to confirm", or turn it into a question. Never write an inference as a fact in a prep document; it will be repeated in the room.

## 5. Creation verbs carry agency

When the candidate created something that did not exist before, lead with Built, Founded, Created, Established, or Launched. "Owned X from zero" is ambiguous: it can mean they inherited an empty project. Leadership-forward verbs (Owned, Led, Drove) suit managing, aligning, and transforming work. Add leadership beats after the creation verb rather than replacing it: "Built the platform from scratch. Secured executive buy-in, set the roadmap, and grew the team from 0 to N."

## 6. Interview-only facts stay out of the CV

`profile/role-context.md` may list facts the candidate is happy to say aloud but not print (exact throughput figures, customer names under NDA, internal metrics). Respect that boundary. Preps may use them; CVs and cover letters may not.
