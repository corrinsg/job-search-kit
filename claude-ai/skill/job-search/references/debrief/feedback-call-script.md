# Feedback call script

Recruiters offer a feedback call after a rejection to close the loop kindly. Left unstructured, the call yields "it was close, the other candidate had slightly more X". Prepared, it yields the specific gaps the panel named, which become lessons. Prepare both directions: what the candidate wants to learn, and any feedback the candidate wants to give on the process.

```
# <Company> | Feedback call with <recruiter>, <date>

## Call structure (15 to 20 minutes)
1. Thank them for the time and the call; say you want specifics so you can improve.
2. Ask your three questions (below).
3. If offering process feedback, give your three or four points.
4. Close: ask about future roles and how to stay in touch.

## What I want to learn
- Which round or rounds carried the decision?
- What specifically did the panel want more of? Ask for an example question where the answer fell short.
- Was there anything in how I ran the case or design round that they would change?

## Process feedback to give (3 or 4 points)
### Point 1 | <topic, e.g. pacing of the decision>
- **Observation:** <what happened, factual, dated>
- **Impact:** <what it caused for me as a candidate>
- **Ask:** <one concrete suggestion>
### Point 2 | <e.g. overlap between rounds>
### Point 3 | <e.g. panel composition>
### Point 4 | <optional>

## Close
- "I would be glad to be considered for future roles on this or adjacent teams. What is the best way to stay on your radar?"
- Ask whether they are comfortable being contacted again in a few months.

## Three delivery techniques
1. Observation before judgement. State what happened, then its effect, then the ask. Do not open with the conclusion.
2. One point at a time, then stop talking. Silence invites detail.
3. Thank them for anything specific they give, even if it is uncomfortable. Specificity is what you came for.

## What not to do
- Do not relitigate an answer or argue with the feedback.
- Do not ask about the other candidate.
- Do not give process feedback that is really disappointment in disguise.
- Do not ask for a second chance on this role; ask about future ones.

## One-line summary (write after the call)
<The single most useful thing learned.>
```

Afterwards, run the debrief step 3 again with the new information and strengthen or add lessons.
