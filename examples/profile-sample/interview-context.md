# Interview context

Sam's own answers, in Sam's words. Prep documents tailor only the company-specific questions; these are drawn from here.

## Why you are looking for a new role
- Freightline consolidated three PM roles into one platform group after the Series C extension; mine was one of them.
- The dispatch rebuild was shipped and adopted, so the timing was clean.
- Looking for the next product where the workflow is broken and the users are professionals who use it every day.

## Your biggest strength
- Finding the real problem by sitting with users. The 40% re-sequencing finding came from two weeks of shadowing, not from a survey, and it reset the roadmap.

## Where you see room for growth
- Committing to a recommendation faster in open-ended discussions. I explore well and sometimes keep exploring when the room wants a call. I now time-box discovery and say "my recommendation is" out loud.

## What you are most proud of professionally
- Planners at a 300-vehicle carrier telling me they stopped coming in 45 minutes early because re-planning no longer needed it.

## A challenge you faced
- Engineering wanted to swap the solver vendor first; I argued for fixing the planner workflow on the existing solver. The tradeoff was about 8% route efficiency for a year. Adoption went from 30% to 70% of carriers, and we swapped the vendor a year later from a position of strength.

## What gets you excited about your work
- Operational software where a small product change shows up in someone's working day within a week.

## What environment helps you do your best work
- Small product team, engineers in the discovery, direct access to customers, leaders who want a recommendation not a menu.

## What you value in leadership
- Clarity on what matters this quarter, and cover to say no to the rest.

## What you are looking for in your next role
- Ownership of a product area with real users, a team to grow, and a domain where operations matter: logistics, field service, supply chain, or similar.

## Where you see yourself in 2 to 3 years
- Leading a product group of three or four PMs in an operational B2B company, still close enough to customers to shadow them.

## Salary approach
- Recruiter screens: ask for the band they have been briefed on before naming a number.
- Anchors if pushed: UK venture-backed B2B SaaS, Senior PM: £95K to £110K base. Lead or Head of Product: £115K to £135K. Non-tech multinationals: expect lower bases and more bonus; check the specific company first.
- Do not quote total compensation figures as base.

## Notice period and availability
- Not currently employed; can start within two weeks of an offer in writing.

## Work authorisation
- British citizen, right to work in the UK, no sponsorship needed.

## Questions you like to ask recruiters
- What is the band you have been briefed on?
- How many stages, and who makes the final call?
- Why is the role open?

## Questions you like to ask hiring managers
- What does a planner's (or your users') worst morning look like today?
- What did the last person in this role get right, and what would you change?
- How do product and engineering disagree here, and how does it get resolved?

## Non-CV differentiators
- Drove a parcel van for a regional courier for a summer at university. Lead with this for logistics roles; it explains the instinct to shadow planners.
- Fluent Spanish; useful for Iberian or Latin American expansion roles.

## Honest gaps and never-claims (across all roles)
- No consumer product experience; do not frame marketplace seller work as consumer.
- Have not managed engineers; say "led a product team" and never "managed the engineering team".
- Not a data scientist; built dashboards and analyses, never models. For roles whose core bar is ML, treat it as a real gap.
