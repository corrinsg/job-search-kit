```markdown
## Negotiation anchors

**Recruiter screen:** do not name a number first. Ask: "Before I share a figure, what is the range you have been briefed on for this role? Then I can tell you whether we are in the same ballpark." Recruiters usually hold a band; hearing it first avoids anchoring below their budget.

**If pushed for a number:** <sector-appropriate base anchor from the table, phrased as "for a <level> role with this scope I would be looking at around <mid to high> base, plus the right bonus and equity mix. Happy to flex on the mix.">

**If they come in below the anchor:** flag it directly: "That is below where I need to land for a role with this scope; is there flex in the band?"

**Later stages:** <what to hold for, what to trade: start date, equity, signing>

## Sources

- <URL, what it showed, date>
```

## Rules that apply when filling this in

- Never invent a number. An empty cell with "no data" is better than a plausible guess.
- Do not read a total-compensation figure as base salary. Levels.fyi and some Glassdoor entries report totals.
- Research the specific company, not the generic title. Pay for the same title differs sharply between venture-backed tech, multinationals in non-tech sectors, listed retailers, and banks.
- Mid-stage startups rarely pay staff-level bases for senior individual-contributor titles. If the range looks high for the stage, check the level mapping.
- Anchoring too high at the screen gets a candidate filtered before the hiring manager ever sees them. Anchor to the market for this company, not to a wish.
- If `profile/interview-context.md` has a salary approach section, follow its anchors and tone; the template above is the default when it is empty.
